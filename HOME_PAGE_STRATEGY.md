# Home Page Strategy: AptyRead Literacy Program

## Is the literacy-program concept good?

**Yes.** Leading with "literacy program" instead of "app" is a strong idea because:

1. **Differentiation** — Parents are tired of "another phonics app." A *program* (4 levels, 243 lessons, one path) signals seriousness and outcomes, not just screen time.
2. **Trust** — "Backed by reading science" and "the kind that used to only exist in specialist schools" positions you as education-first, not entertainment.
3. **Clarity** — "Speaking English ≠ reading English" is a message that resonates with both native and ESL families. It names the problem and positions AptyRead as the solution.
4. **Conversion** — Parents and educators look for *programs* they can recommend or adopt; "app" feels optional, "program" feels like a curriculum.

---

## Connecting with parents

- **Lead with the question:** "But can they read?" — Use it in the hero or right after. It creates a moment of recognition.
- **Speak to their doubt:** Address "knows ABCs but can't read," "speaks well but can't read," "slow learner" in copy or a short FAQ strip so they feel seen.
- **One clear journey:** Emphasize "one path, start to finish" and "at their own pace" so they feel no pressure and no dead ends.
- **Low risk:** Keep "Try 4 free lessons" and "no registration" prominent. Everyone starts at the start; no level-checking or skipping (foundation first).
- **Avoid character focus:** Dropping the AI girl and focusing on the *program* (lessons, levels, outcomes) keeps the tone professional and parent-trust oriented.

---

## Connecting with educators

- **One line in the header or hero:** e.g. "For parents & educators" or "Used by families and schools" so educators know they’re included.
- **Dedicated short section or footer line:** "Schools & tutors: AptyRead is a complete literacy program you can recommend or use in class. [Contact / Learn more]." This can be a single block or a link to a future /educators or /schools page.
- **Curriculum language:** Use "4 levels," "243 lessons," "reading science," "mastery-based" — the same terms schools use when choosing programs.
- **Later:** A separate /educators or /schools page with bulk licensing, placement, and outcomes would deepen educator adoption; the homepage only needs to signal that educators are part of the audience.

---

## Recommended homepage changes (implemented)

1. **Hero** — Lead with "AptyRead Literacy Program" and the core idea: from first sound to first book; optional "But can they read?" subhead. CTA: "Start Your Child's Reading Journey" (and keep "Try 4 Free Lessons"). Remove or replace any AI-character imagery (e.g. swap `parent-child-reading.png` if it shows the AI girl).
2. **Why this works** — Remove "Apty" and "AI teacher"; reframe as a *structured program* (e.g. "A complete, structured program. Concept, then practice, then done—like a real reading class.").
3. **The 4 levels** — Align with your program: Level 1 Learning the Sounds (46 lessons), Level 2 Reading First Words (57), Level 3 Reading Anything (63), Level 4 Reading Real Books (77). Include ages and one-line outcomes so parents and educators see the full journey.
4. **Social proof / mission** — Add a short "Our mission" line: "No child should fall behind because they couldn't read." Optionally a "For educators" line or small block.
5. **CTA** — Primary: "Start Your Child's Reading Journey" or "Try 4 Free Lessons"; secondary: "Find your child's level" (link to app or a simple quiz/level guide if you have it).
6. **Structured data** — Describe the site as a literacy program (e.g. "AptyRead Literacy Program — complete reading program, 4 levels, 243 lessons") so search results match the new positioning.

---

## Audience priority

- **Primary:** Children 3 to 10.
- **Secondary:** Parents (supporting their child).
- **Next:** Educators (recommend or use the program).

**Tagline:** From First Sound to Fluent Reader (do not change).

**Structure:** Nobody can join Level 2 or higher directly. Level checking is not possible. Without the correct foundation, children cannot move on — so every child builds the right base first (avoids someone joining Level 4 and saying the program isn’t effective).

---

## Summary

- **Concept:** Strong. Lead with "literacy program," not "app."
- **Tagline:** From First Sound to Fluent Reader.
- **Audience:** Children 3–10 first, then parents, then educators.
- **No skipping levels:** Messaging and copy stress foundation-first; no direct entry to Level 2+.
- **Implementation:** Hero and levels aligned to level cards (parent quotes, pills, no-skip messaging); educators page clarifies audience order and structure.
