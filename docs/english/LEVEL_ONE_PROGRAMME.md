# English programme: Level 1 and the next four levels

Status: authored web prototype, 30 September 2026. This is the current content contract for `lib/english-programme.ts`; it supersedes the forty-lesson packaging proposal, not the underlying v0.3 literacy principles. No child-study, effectiveness, pronunciation approval or mastery claim follows from authoring or software checks. New teaching videos are explicitly placeholders; complete narration/audio/visual review before production release.

## What Level 1 teaches

The core route contains **10 lessons**: the existing SAT and PIN lessons, a first tiny book, six more correspondence groups, and a finite completion bridge. The new programme file supplies **41 activities** alongside the 24 original SAT/PIN activities: **65 core activities**. The separate formation area covers 24 letters with 48 uppercase/lowercase guides in 54 optional topics. A lesson is a collection of short stopping points, not a required sitting. New activities usually contain 2–4 trials; a book has 3–4 pages. Confirm actual duration with children rather than advertising a fixed dose.

| Core lesson | New code | Immediate modelled words | Reading/language application |
|---|---|---|---|
| Our first words | s, short a, t | at, sat | Known-word recognition, sitting meaning, spoken-word encoding |
| More little words | p, short i, n | tap after p; sit after i; pin; existing pan/tap | Contrast short a/i; reuse earlier sounds and meanings |
| Pat and Pip | No new code | Revisit pin/sit | Pat sat. / Sit, Pip. / Pip sat.; invitation, agent/action, event order |
| Play together | hard c, m, short e | cat, mat, pen | Pat/Pip can tap; can means being able to do something |
| A little race | h, r, hard g | hat, rat, pig | Sam/Pat ran; Sam sat; past events and who did what |
| Dig and rest | d, k, short o | sad, kid, dog | Kim/Pat can dig; getting hot and resting; simple cause |
| Find the bag | l, f, b | log, fan, bag | Picture information captions; bag on bed/in bin/on mat; in versus on |
| A wet day | short u, j, w | sun, jug, wet | Jim gets wet, runs, sits, has fun; sequence and feelings |
| Meet Zed | v, consonant y, z | van, yam, zip | A named dog can yap, runs, gets wet, sits; cause and order |
| Completion bridge | No new code | Bounded familiar-word sample | One short narrative and one caption book; purpose and meaning |

These are 24 initial correspondences, not the whole English code or a complete alphabet course. x and q/u remain later sound-sequence work; long vowels, soft c/g, consonant teams, suffixes and clusters are not silently introduced in child-read print. Names and capitals are prepared before their books. The two information books contain explicitly identified captions, not claims of full sentence reading. Article a, pronoun I, is and the are absent from independently offered print here; rich spoken stories may contain them.

## Activity contract

- Retrieve something familiar, explicitly model one new correspondence, use it immediately in a word, then offer a short child-first print opportunity with help.
- A guided build shows the target and may cue its next letter. An encoding check supplies the spoken word without printed answer or advance correct-tile cue. Record help/retries as supported.
- A `read` task initially shows print alone. Reveal pictures and their spoken labels after the child’s attempt; hearing a model remains available. A correct picture choice is a constrained meaning response, not proof of spoken decoding.
- A book prepares its names, relevant uppercase links, meanings and print conventions. Each page offers print before illustration/model. Hearing prepared text means the book is practised/supported material, not an unseen transfer assessment.
- Listening stories use richer language than the child-read print. Distinguish listening understanding, picture-supported meaning and observed reading. Keep short opportunities to say, act or retell without pretending to measure unobserved speech.
- Each new-code group mixes an earlier sound, a new word, an earlier word and a small spelling opportunity. A later activity is a useful revisit; it is not automatically a delayed check on another day.
- Individual sounds use recorded phonemes. Device speech may model whole words/instructions during this prototype; review naturalness and pronunciation before recording/publishing final media.
- The renderer must show a small target-plus-distractors set from taught letters, not every known letter. Picture choice positions vary deterministically. Help is available without a failure loop.
- Stop and return at an activity boundary; keep partial progress and completed replays. Each newly introduced letter has a visible, optional formation invitation inside its existing topic. Keep reading bypasses drawing; actual forms tried are recorded separately from reading completion. All 48 completed handwriting videos are available alongside the animation and paper practice. Neither tracing accuracy nor completing all formation topics gates new reading code. See [opening journey refinement](OPENING_JOURNEY_REFINEMENT.md) for persistence and presentation details.

## Exposure, evidence and progression

Keep access/completion separate from evidence. The programme records only observable sound selections, tile encoding and constrained meaning selections, with support and a date. “I tried it”, page turns, celebration, duration and voice playback do not verify oral accuracy or fluent reading. “Independent” on a response means no help/retry in that response, not fresh-item transfer, broad word knowledge or reading mastery.

The original reserved observation set includes nap/tip and pan/tin. **Pan is already taught in this web route**, so it is exposed and must not reappear as an unseen item. This programme preserves nap, tip and tin from child-read tasks. Any future check needs a versioned exposure register shared with other implementations; known letters alone do not certify an item as fresh.

Legacy topic IDs and saved work are retained. Added activities have stable IDs and explicit insertion points. Migrated numeric positions must resolve to the same old activity ID. Optional formation completion remains available without forcing a formerly completed learner to redo writing. A parent preview must be transient and must never write fabricated completion or evidence into a child’s stored record.

The completion bridge ends Level 1 participation after a bounded word sample and two short reading experiences. It does not require endless review or a perfect oral performance. Celebrate exploration, offer replay and explain the next level. Before making an independent-reading claim, collect an optional observed sample on matched print and another occasion; uncertainty or absence of a listener stays unknown. The original assessment specification proposes later-day confirmation and targeted review; route completion alone does not implement either.

## Small milestones and child review

1. First correspondences: child understands listen/tap/help and can participate in SAT tasks.
2. Six-letter words: child has opportunities to blend, encode and connect meaning; support and observation source are explicit.
3. Several vowels: child encounters cumulative words across a/i/e/o/u rather than only rehearsing one ending.
4. Connected print: child tries short matched sentences and captions, answers a constrained meaning question, and can return later.

Before expanding/releasing: observe children aged 4–5 and 6–7, including English learners, independently locating the next activity, replaying a sound, responding without an adult paraphrase, using help, leaving/resuming, and retelling/showing one event. Inspect sound/picture confusions, repeated guesses and fatigue. Check on a second day with a matched but unrehearsed item if the aim is transfer. Use these observations to adjust activity length and supports; do not select an arbitrary completion-percentage mastery cutoff.

## Level 2 boundary: first reading with meaning

Entry is brief routing from actual short-word/task-language evidence; do not replay the complete alphabet automatically. Targeted earlier support can continue beside new work. The v0.3 map has ten authoring blocks, which are not fixed app lessons:

| Block | Scope |
|---|---|
| S2.01 | Brief placement: reading, spelling and instruction understanding separately |
| S2.02 | Short a across positions/endings; explicitly teach article a when used |
| S2.03 | Short i versus a; location in; explicitly introduce is if needed |
| S2.04 | Short o with a/i; on versus in; explicitly teach the if used |
| S2.05 | Short e, especially e/i contrasts; description and location |
| S2.06 | Short u across five vowels; negation meaning |
| S2.07 | Sound order, changes and spoken-word spelling; separate from a reading access gate |
| S2.08 | Phrases/sentences, purposeful grouping, pronouns/articles; model final nd before and |
| S2.09 | Small narrative and factual text; literal meaning, sequence, simple cause |
| S2.10 | Fresh matched text, purposeful rereading and later new-text transfer |

Sentences, spelling and meaning occur throughout, not only in the final blocks. Exit profile: fresh mixed VC/CVC words, represented sounds in spelling, a short matched unfamiliar text with understood meaning; oral accuracy only where observed. This is a future authoring map, not 10 existing production lessons.

## Level 3 boundary: letter teams and longer sound sequences

Keep digraphs (one sound) distinct from adjacent consonants (separate sounds). Introduce one correspondence or suffix pronunciation at a time within each block. Earlier code matters only where the selected item uses it.

Entry depends on the actual text or word, not completion of every preceding block. Maintain richer oral knowledge and language alongside reading.

| Block | Scope |

| S5.08 | ful, less, ly, ness, ment; comparative er/est, separately |
| S5.09 | ph, tch, dge, selected kn/wr/mb, ch for /k/, separately |
| S5.10 | Selected tion, sion, ture and their pronunciations |
| S5.11 | Additional vowels: all/want/word/bear/cold/put/son families, word-led review |
| S5.12 | ie, ey, eigh and selected ei alternatives |
| S5.13 | Common contractions and apostrophe meaning |
| S5.14 | Monitor sentence meaning, reference and connectives |
| S5.15 | Fresh narrative: goals/events/outcome, evidence-supported inference, phrasing |

Course exit is scoped to reviewed matched fiction/information: read, understand, handle a difficulty, and show later transfer with the evidence source stated. It is not mastery of every English spelling or adult text. Continued reading, vocabulary, knowledge and selected rare-pattern support follow the finite course.
