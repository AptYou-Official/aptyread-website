# AptyRead English — proposed Level 1 lesson sequence

Discussion draft, 28 September 2026. Not approved for production. No live curriculum, navigation, audio manifest or media configuration is changed by this document.

## Starting point

Preserve the implemented first five lessons and their 43 topics. Main P/I/N teaching videos are still pending. Level 1 introduces first sound–letter connections, blending, word construction, meaning and early matched text. It does not certify general reading fluency.

The working plan is 40 short lessons, not 40 sessions or days. Counts and packaging may change after authoring and child/teacher review. Each new correspondence gets its own small teaching step inside its group.

## Teaching principles

- Keep the six-topic Explore template: big/small video, find both forms, capital-writing video, capital practice, lowercase-writing video, lowercase practice. Adapt stroke order, shape and known-letter choices; do not copy sound or letter-specific explanations blindly.
- Retain the current sequential route and replay. Participation/completion is distinct from verified reading. Paper writing remains an alternative, not a compulsory gate. Any future accelerated route requires a separate design decision.
- New-sound lessons retrieve a few older sounds as well as the target. Only taught alternatives appear as choices.
- Build and read words, explain their meanings, then hear and construct words without a printed answer. Guided copying and sound-to-letter construction serve different purposes.
- Gradually move from an explicit model to a child-first reading opportunity with help available. Do not automatically play the answer or show a revealing picture before that opportunity.
- Revisit earlier words among new ones; do not only rehearse one ending. A word-change task can be useful but is not a mandatory readiness hurdle.
- Extra practice is small and purposeful. Neither all words in a bank nor perfect independent oral reading becomes a new access lock.
- Short, demonstrated, replayable directions and rich oral language support are essential for ESL learners. Parents can add home-language discussion, but ordinary use should not depend on it.
- Five reading stars appreciate participation. Self-reported reading and supported reading must not be reported as observed independent fluency.

## Books and sentences

Lesson 9 begins with a shared word book: the child reads known words; richer narration supplies the surrounding story. Distinguish those roles. Do not label narration-supported reading as independent story reading.

As code and language permit, move into actual sentences and small coherent texts, not only isolated words. After C/M/E, examples such as “Sam sat.” and “Pat can tap.” can be considered: explicitly introduce character names, capital links, can's meaning and sentence punctuation. These examples are not a finished story.

Reading application lessons should gradually include both small stories and simple information or instruction texts. They are concentrated application opportunities; reading and meaning also continue inside new-sound lessons and the replay library.

Every final child-read text needs the original map's full print/language ledger: sound–spelling mappings, structures, endings, names, meanings, grammar, punctuation, pronunciation variants and support. Known letters alone are insufficient. Article a, pronoun I, is and the require explicit treatment if used before their code is known; do not silently insert them. Apty can appear in illustrations and narration without being assumed independently decodable.

## Immediate next production proposal

Author Explore P, Explore I and Explore N, then the first word book. Finalize that book's child-read words, spoken story and support record before recording or building it. Continue with C/M/E after reviewing this milestone with children and teachers.

Pin and sit remain the immediate pronunciation-clip priorities. Pan and tap are optional useful additions. Do not commission a separate mouth video for every candidate word.

## Research basis and limits

The IES/What Works Clearinghouse practice guide supports connecting speech sounds to print, early decoding and word building, explicit language development and connected-text practice. Its classroom recommendations inform this proposal; they do not validate this app, the exact letter order, four-word allocation or 40-lesson count.
